About John Silverton

John Silverton is the author of the Dr. Jack Lambert geopolitical thrillers, blending espionage, medicine, and international conflict.

Born and raised in the UK, John moved to the United States in 1976 and now lives in California. A retired plastic surgeon, he spends his time writing, painting in oils, and rowing, while enjoying travel with his wife and time with his family and Border Collies.

His novels explore the hidden worlds of intelligence, power, and consequence—where information can be more dangerous than bullets.

John Silverton's Perspective

John Silverton's novels are a testament to his unique perspective as a former plastic surgeon and his deep understanding of the human psyche. His stories are filled with intricate plots, compelling characters, and thought-provoking themes that challenge readers to think critically about the world around them.

Through his writing, John aims to entertain, educate, and inspire readers to explore the complexities of human nature and the consequences of our actions in an ever-changing global landscape.

Thoughts on Artificial Intelligence

I will admit it: I utilized artificial intelligence while crafting my first work of fiction. However, it did not create the story itself. The characters, plot, themes, and decisions that shaped this novel are entirely my own. What AI provided was something I lacked—a writing coach.

As a first-time fiction writer, I had much to learn. I needed assistance understanding the language and procedures used by police officers and intelligence personnel, as well as guidance on Washington, D.C., and the workings of government.

Most importantly, I needed to master the craft of storytelling.

Artificial intelligence helped me discern when to use description and when to keep paragraphs concise. It taught me techniques for building tension, creating atmosphere, revealing character through action, and ending chapters in ways that encourage readers to turn the page. Like any student, I learned by asking questions, studying examples, and applying what I learned to my own work.

I treated AI much as I would have treated an experienced writing instructor. I asked questions, studied techniques, experimented, rewrote scenes, and gradually developed my own style. As the manuscript progressed, I relied less on AI and more on the skills I had acquired.

I developed my own style and voice. When AI-generated text appeared in the manuscript, I revised and edited it extensively to ensure it matched that voice. One of the first lessons I learned was to remove the abundance of em dashes that AI seems so fond of.

I understand that some writers are skeptical of artificial intelligence. That is their right. My experience was different. In the absence of a local writing group or a human mentor, AI became a patient teacher—available whenever I needed guidance and always familiar with the project at hand.

This novel is ultimately my work, but it is also the product of a learning journey, and artificial intelligence played a meaningful role in that journey.

I have always enjoyed oil painting and developed my own style without mentors. Through trial and error I learned about perspective, colors, and how to balance the picture. Then AI came along. It substituted for a real human painting coach, teaching me about grisaille, or grey scale underpainting often used by the old masters, and how to use fine transparent washes and glazes to achieve complex deeper effects. I found this very helpful in the sea and ocean scenes that I am fond of.

AI became my painting mentor, providing constructive criticism of my art, improving my skills, and being a great help.

AI did not write my book any more than it was unable to actually create my paintings. AI was my mentor in both skills.

AI is different from a human in that it is always up to speed with my creations, available at any time, and gives almost instant feedback, and costs nothing as long as I restrict the amount of time I use AI. For that, I am grateful.
